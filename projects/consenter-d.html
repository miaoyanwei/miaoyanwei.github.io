Making web consent easier to understand

I designed the core flows for Consenter, a privacy toolkit that helps people understand data requests, decide what to share, and reuse their choices on compatible websites.

What makes a data request worth trusting?

Data exposure incidents had eroded trust in Europe. At the same time, some cookie banners steered visitors toward accepting before they understood what they were agreeing to.

In earlier research, people who were wary of sharing data were more willing to consent when a specialist sat beside them and explained the purpose, benefits, and risks.

That led to our design question: how could a website give someone that same context at the point of choice?

What an informed choice needs

Education
Understand why a site asks for data, and what the benefits and risks are.
Transparency
Find supporting details and know when something changes.
Autonomy
Decline, accept, or change a choice with comparable effort.
Convenience
Reuse preferences on compatible sites to reduce repeat prompts.

Consenter Banner

Explain the request where people decide

Problem

A visitor can dismiss a prompt in a second. Understanding why the site wants data takes longer, especially when the explanation is buried.

Design response

I worked on putting the purpose, benefits, and risks beside the choice. A request to improve the website can explain the benefit of better usability and the risk that analytics records how people browse.

Consenter Manager

Give operators a way to explain what they collect

Problem

A banner can only be as specific as the information behind it. Two sites can ask to improve their service while using different tools and collecting different data.

Design response

I designed the Manager’s information architecture around purposes, technologies, and legal details. The operator’s configuration informs the benefits and risks shown in the Banner. The underlying detail stays accessible for anyone who wants to check it.

Consenter Agent

Show where a saved choice will apply

Problem

Answering the same question on every website is tiring. Saving a broader preference also creates a new question: where will it apply, and can I change it later?

Design response

“Save for this site only” and “Save for all sites” make the scope explicit. The broader option introduces the Agent, which can reuse preferences on compatible sites. People can review and change their choices in the Agent.

What shipped and what I learned

Three MVPs went live

We released MVPs of Consenter Agent, Consenter Banner, and Consenter Manager. The Chrome extension became available on 18 December 2025.

Our broader ambition was to make informed consent a practical industry standard shaped by European privacy requirements.
